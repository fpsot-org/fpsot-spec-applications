= Jurisdiction profiles
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

The intake rules that the research compared share their mechanics and split on every legal value.
Federal law extends a time limit that falls on a holiday to the next day that is not a holiday.
Québec extends it to the next working day, and its list of holidays differs from the federal list.
Québec presumes when an electronic document arrives, and federal law leaves that to each program.
Québec asks a business to apply for a permit in French only.
Federal law gives a choice of two languages.

No single rule could serve all of them.
This page therefore keeps each legal value and each legal rule in a jurisdiction profile, apart from the core rules.
A profile states the practice of {abbr-fpsot-first}, and it is not legal advice.

== Profile files

[[APP-010]]
*APP-010.* Each {term-deploying-body} MUST name the profiles whose law applies to each of its services.
Each profile MUST be a file of the class `ApplicationProfile` of `model/applications.yaml`, in the folder `profiles/`.
Each profile MUST list its sources.
For each source, it MUST state whether the research read it, or mark it `to-be-verified`.
A source read in an archived copy counts as read only when that copy holds the text in force.

[cols="2,3,4",options="header"]
|===
|Profile |Applies to |Content of this release
|`core` |Every application |The defaults of APP-014.
|`ca-federal` |Applications to federal bodies of Canada |The holidays and the roll to the next day that is not a holiday, standard time, real-time status and service standards, both official languages, direct collection and the retention floor of two years, and receipt time set by each program.
|`ca-qc` |Applications to Québec public bodies |The holidays and the roll to the next working day, the presumption of receipt of electronic documents, the chance to complete a file, French by default with French only for the permits and grants of a business, the notice at collection, official data sources for prefill, and the accessibility standard.
|===

A French profile does not exist in this release.
The research read the French code only in archived copies of 2024 and 2025.
The file `jurisdictions/fr.yaml` therefore describes France without a normative profile.

The `ca-federal` profile follows the Interpretation Act and the Directive on Service and Digital.
It also follows the Official Languages Act and the Privacy Act.
Its section 26 moves a time limit that falls on a holiday to the next day that is not one.
Section 35 lists the holidays, and Sunday is among them, while Saturday is not.
The federal law on electronic documents lets each program set by regulation when a document counts as received.
The profile therefore holds no default time of receipt.

The `ca-qc` profile follows the Québec Interpretation Act and the Québec law on information technology.
It follows the Act respecting administrative justice and the Charter of the French language.
It also follows the Québec law on access to documents, and the law on the information resources of public bodies.
The research read the French text of each at LégisQuébec, current to 2026-10-15.

Article 52 of the Québec act moves a time limit that ends on a holiday to the next working day.
Whether a Saturday is a working day outside the land register is _to be verified_.
The profile therefore skips Saturdays as well.

== Sources, dates and versions

[[APP-011]]
*APP-011.* Each rule and each value of a profile MUST cite one of the sources of that profile.
Each dated value, such as a holiday, MUST carry the dates to which it applies.
Each profile MUST have its own version number under Semantic Versioning 2.0.0, apart from the version of this specification.
A release that removes or relaxes a value of the same profile MUST take a new major version.

The holidays of 2027 are then a profile release, not a change to the rules.
The decisions specification dates its parameters in the same way, under DEC-037.

== No law in the core

[[APP-012]]
*APP-012.* A service MUST take each calendar and each legal time zone from a profile.
It MUST take each rule on language and each rule on the time of receipt from a profile.
It MUST take each period of acknowledgment, each retention floor and each basis for prefill from a profile too.
It MUST NOT hard-code one.
A profile MUST NOT relax a MUST of the core rules.

A service declaration holds the values of one program, such as its clocks.
A profile holds the law that many programs share.
The same service then takes in a federal license and a Québec permit, each with its own profile.

== Missing values

[[APP-013]]
*APP-013.* A rule may need a value that neither the profile, the service declaration nor `core` gives for a date.
An example is a calendar that holds no holidays for the year of a deadline.
The service MUST NOT then compute the date or apply the rule.
It MUST refer the file to a person, with a code that names the missing value.
It MUST NOT assume a value.

A silent default would turn a gap in a profile into a deadline that nobody set.
The calendars of the examples end on 1 January 2027 for that reason.

== Defaults

[[APP-014]]
*APP-014.* Each service MUST also follow the `core` profile, which sets these defaults.

* An attachment field that declares no limit accepts files of 10 MiB at most, under APP-060.
* An attachment field that declares no types accepts PDF, JPEG and PNG files only.
* The digest of every file and every submission uses SHA-256, under APP-062.
* The service seals each receipt record at least under the profile `seal-log` of the signed records specification, under APP-042.
* A notice goes to the applicant at least 14 days before the service purges a draft, under APP-034.

A service declaration MAY lower the size limit or narrow the types of a field.
It MAY raise the size limit of a field when the program needs larger files, and it states why.
It MUST NOT choose another digest algorithm.
